    How the home flows

      Space check

      The walkway is measured at every step along the visitor path, straight across the direction people walk, so the turns and the island tables are checked too. The target is at least 6′-0″ everywhere, so larger groups keep flowing without holding up the people behind them.

      6′-0″ or more groups flow freely 5′-0″ to 6′-0″ pairs pass, groups bunch under 5′-0″ too narrow, people queue
      • Assumed depths: a person at a table, counter or tablet needs 2′-6″; someone browsing the wardrobe 3′-0″; people reading a panel 1′-6″; volunteers serving from behind a table 2′-0″; people watching the instrument lessons 1′-0″. In the ghar mandir, offering petals happens at the end of the middle walkway, where it splits, so that spot is counted as walkway and the two branches are checked around it.
      • ADA requires at least 3′-0″ of clear route and 5′-0″ for a wheelchair to turn; every walkway here is wider than both. The front door is 8 ft, inner doorways 9′-6″ (P1) and 7′-6″ (P2, P3), and the exit 7′-9″.
      • Walls are 8′-0″ tall. Panels hang from 1′-6″ to 7′-6″. There are no banners above the walls; the HAPPY and DIWALI banners hang down the outside of the house front from 8′-6″ to 1′-6″. Adult eye height is about 5′-3″; a child’s is about 3′-9″.

      Notes before you build

      • Footprint. 40 × 24 ft so every outer wall is whole 8 × 4 ft panels: 10 along the north and south walls, 4 on the east wall either side of the door, 4 on the west wall. Inner dividers are cut to whatever width they need.
      • One exit. Visitors leave the ghar mandir through the 7′-9″ exit on the left of the mandir, at the south end of the west wall. Confirm with the Manyata team that their entrance lines up with it.
      • Windows in the house front. Cut each 3 × 3′-6″ window into a wall flat before it is faced, and add a 2×4 header and sill inside the frame so the flat stays rigid. The foam frames and chhajjas glue and screw onto the outer face.
      • No open flames. LED diyas at the front door and flower petals at the ghar mandir. String lights should be UL-listed and plugged into switched power strips. Ask the venue and fire marshal what they require (flame-retardant plywood or FR paint, clear exits, exit signs and egress lights left on).
      • Freestanding walls. Built the same way as Mulyo. The outer walls stand on jacks with sandbags. P2 and P3 are each two 8 ft pieces (2 whole flats) either side of a 7′-6″ center doorway; P1 is a 12 ft piece (3 whole flats) tied to the north wall with a 2 ft half-flat return against the south wall, leaving a 9′-6″ doorway. Every divider piece gets T-feet at its doorway end. The west wall is one 16 ft piece from the north wall, ending at the exit: brace its free end. The door façade adds weight to the east wall, so brace both sides of the front door.
      • Keep the walkway clear. Nothing stands on the walkway side of a room: no panels, props or volunteers. The floater in the bedroom and the host in the ghar mandir keep groups moving.
      • Outside the footprint. The rangoli, planters, greeters and shoe rack use an apron of about 4 ft on the main hall side of the front door. Confirm that space is free with the layout team.
      • Food safety. Only pre-packed sweets and snacks, with allergen labels. The tasting server wears gloves and keeps a bin at the dining room exit. Check with the mandir’s food-safety coordinator.
      • Ghar mandir. Coordinate the mandir, murtis and decoration with the women’s overall centerpiece work. Collect offered petals respectfully at the end of each day and refresh them every morning.
      • Power. Outlets are needed for the 5 iPads (living room south wall), the bhajan speaker (tabla rug), the string lights, the seven lamps and the URTOM wall spots and uplights (one power strip per room, cords dropped down the back of the wall). Only the picture bars are cordless; they need a charging point overnight. Tape every cord down at the wall base so nothing crosses the walkway.
      • Still to confirm. The Diwali phrase for the listening wall and the 8–10 languages and voices to record it. Gujarati and music class details for the brochures. Who lends the sofa, wardrobe, tables and instruments, with names on every borrowed item.

      Light schedule

      • Positions are measured from the north wall (on dividers and the east wall) or from the east end (on the north and south walls). Screw each spot's metal bracket to the wall, aim it at the point listed, then fine-tune on site.
      • Spot cords: drill a ½ in hole beside each spot and drop the cord down the back of the flat to the room's power strip, so no cord shows on the painted face. Leave each foot switch on and use the strip's switch to turn a whole room on or off.
      • Picture bars: set every bar to 2700K so they sit with the 3000K spots, and to 70% brightness to stretch the battery through a full day. Lift them off the magnetic mount to charge overnight (USB-C), and keep the spare charged to swap in. One remote controls them all, and the auto-off timer (2/4/6/8 h) saves battery if a room is left on.
      • The lamps and string lights plug in; run their cords along the wall base, never across the walkway.

      Paint · 5 colours, no split panels

      Wall colours

      Five paints in total. The living room and bedroom share linen white walls with a soft sage accent along the north wall; the dining room and ghar mandir share soft sand walls with a deep maroon accent along the south wall and behind the mandir. Every outside-facing face, including the house front, is sandstone. Each room pair starts and ends on whole 4 ft flats, so no panel is painted in two colours. Take the hex codes to the paint store; eggshell finish, 2 coats over primer.
