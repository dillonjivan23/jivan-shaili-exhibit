      Spotlight plan

      The gym lights stay off, so every display is lit by its own spotlight. This is what the lighting team asked each section for: where each light goes and how many we need. All spots are warm 3000K clamp lamps on the wall tops at 8′-9″, on short arms, the same fixture as Mulyo so they can be ordered together. The two island tables are lit from the divider beside them, so no light shines across a walkway. Numbers match the plan below and the labels in the 3D view (turn on Exhibit lighting).

      Space check

      The walkway is measured at every step along the visitor path, straight across the direction people walk, so the turns and the island tables are checked too. The target is at least 6′-0″ everywhere, so larger groups keep flowing without holding up the people behind them.

      6′-0″ or more groups flow freely 5′-0″ to 6′-0″ pairs pass, groups bunch under 5′-0″ too narrow, people queue
      • Assumed depths: a person at a table, counter or tablet needs 2′-6″; someone browsing the wardrobe 3′-0″; devotees offering petals 2′-6″; people reading a panel 1′-6″; volunteers serving from behind a table 2′-0″; people watching the instrument lessons 1′-6″.
      • ADA requires at least 3′-0″ of clear route and 5′-0″ for a wheelchair to turn; every walkway here is wider than both, and every doorway is 7′-6″.
      • Walls are 8′-0″ tall. Panels hang from 1′-6″ to 7′-6″, and header banners sit above the walls from 8′-3″ to 10′-0″. Adult eye height is about 5′-3″; a child’s is about 3′-9″.

      Notes before you build

      • Bigger footprint. Four 15 ft rooms make the section 60 × 20 ft instead of 40 × 20 ft. The exhibition layout map still shows 40 × 20, so confirm the extra 20 ft with the layout team before building.
      • No open flames. LED diyas at the front door and flower petals at the ghar mandir. String lights should be UL-listed and plugged into switched power strips. Ask the venue and fire marshal what they require (flame-retardant plywood or FR paint, clear exits, exit signs and egress lights left on).
      • Freestanding walls. Built the same way as Mulyo. The outer walls stand on jacks with sandbags. Dividers P1 and P3 are single 12 ft pieces tied to one outer wall, with their doorways against the other; P2 is two 6 ft pieces either side of a center doorway. Every divider gets T-feet at its doorway end. The door façade adds weight to the east wall, so brace both sides of the front door.
      • Keep the walkway clear. Nothing stands on the walkway side of a room: no panels, props or volunteers. The floater in the bedroom and the exit guide in the ghar mandir keep groups moving.
      • Outside the footprint. The rangoli, greeters, shoe rack and lights L01–L03 use an apron of about 4 ft on the main hall side of the front door. Confirm that space is free with the layout team.
      • Food safety. Only pre-packed sweets and snacks, with allergen labels. The tasting server wears gloves and keeps a bin at the dining room exit. Check with the mandir’s food-safety coordinator.
      • Ghar mandir. Coordinate the mandir, murtis and decoration with the women’s overall centerpiece work. Collect offered petals respectfully at the end of each day and refresh them every morning.
      • Power. Outlets are needed for the 3 tablets (living room south wall), the bhajan speaker and string lights (ghar mandir), and the spotlights on every wall. Tape every cord down at the wall base so nothing crosses the walkway.
      • Still to confirm. The Diwali phrase for the listening wall and the 8–10 languages and voices to record it. Mehndi booth yes or no. Gujarati and music class details for the brochures. Who lends the sofa, wardrobe, tables and instruments, with names on every borrowed item.
